How the Model Works
Summary for senior stakeholders
This model assigns each credit card customer a probability of defaulting in the following month. Customers are grouped into four risk tiers, each with a defined response. Rather than reviewing all 30,000 customers, the risk team can focus on the top 30% flagged by the model and identify 62.3% of all defaulters — more than twice the coverage achieved through random selection.
At the current threshold of 0.50, the model catches 705 defaulters and protects NT$94.5M in capital. A further 622 defaulters are missed, representing NT$83.3M in avoidable losses. Lowering the threshold recovers more of these — at the cost of flagging additional good customers. This is a business decision.

Strongest Predictor of Default
What drives risk in this portfolio
The most important predictor is payment delay history — specifically, the average number of months a customer has been late across the past six months. The most recent month (September) carries the highest individual weight, confirming that recent behaviour is more predictive than older history. Credit limit and bill amounts have comparatively weak predictive power on their own.
Customers who are 1–2 months late represent the most cost-effective intervention point. Contacting them before they reach Very High Risk is significantly cheaper than recovering defaulted debt.

Response by Tier
Recommended actions for the risk team
Very High Risk — 941 customers
Immediate contact required. Decline new credit applications. Request collateral or guarantor for existing facilities. Consider restructuring conversations before default occurs. 59.6% of this group is expected to default.
High Risk — 1,065 customers
Reduce credit limit by 30–50%. Do not approve limit increases. Flag for monthly review. If the customer contacts the bank, offer a payment plan. Any further payment delay should trigger escalation to Very High Risk.
Medium Risk — 2,210 customers
Approve without upselling. Review quarterly. If the most recent payment status deteriorates by one month, escalate to High Risk immediately. These customers are the most likely to tip under adverse economic conditions.
Low Risk — 1,784 customers
Approve and consider for limit increases or product upsell. Annual review is sufficient. These customers represent the lowest credit risk in the portfolio.

Limitations & Governance
What must be addressed before this model is used in production
This model was developed as part of an MSc FinTech project using 2005 Taiwanese credit data. The methodology is sound and the results are directionally valid, but the model requires retraining on current data before any production deployment.
Model Limitations
Training Data is From 2005
Consumer payment behaviour, interest rate environments, and credit products have changed substantially. The model must be retrained on current data before deployment.
Market-Specific to Taiwan
The Taiwanese credit market has different regulatory requirements, customer behaviour, and risk characteristics. This model cannot be applied in the UK, EU, or other markets without full revalidation.
Performance During Economic Shocks
The model is trained on stable economic conditions. During crises such as rapid rate increases, recessions, or financial shocks, historical payment patterns may break down and model performance will degrade.
Probability Calibration
The predicted probabilities have not been validated against observed default rates. Calibration is required before the outputs can be used in pricing models or regulatory capital calculations.
No Macroeconomic Inputs
The model relies entirely on customer-level payment history. It has no awareness of interest rate changes, unemployment levels, or GDP — all of which materially affect default rates at portfolio level.
Regulatory & Fairness
Gender Variable Must Be Removed
SEX is the 5th most important feature in the model. Using gender in credit decisions violates equal credit opportunity regulations in most jurisdictions including the UK and EU. This variable must be excluded before production. The expected reduction in AUC is less than 0.5%.
Marital Status — Legal Review Required
MARRIAGE appears in the top 10 predictive features. Depending on jurisdiction, a disparate impact assessment may be required before this variable can be used in credit decisions.
Individual Decision Explanations — SHAP
The model uses SHAP values to produce a clear explanation for every individual credit decision. If a customer or regulator requests the reasoning behind a decline or flag, the bank can provide a human-readable breakdown of the factors involved. This satisfies the GDPR right to explanation.
